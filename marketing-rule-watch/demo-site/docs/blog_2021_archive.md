# Staying the course when markets wobble

March 15, 2021 · Tomas Wrenfield

A year after the pandemic sell-off, a lot of our conversations with clients have shifted from "should we get out?" to "did we miss the rally?" Both questions come from the same place: the feeling that someone, somewhere, knew what was going to happen. Nobody did.

## What a down year actually looks like

It helps to remember what a bad year felt like before 2020. Nearly every client account we managed ended 2018 with a loss, and our largest decline that year was about 7%. Those clients who stayed invested were back above their starting balances by the end of 2019.

We do not share that to scare anyone. Declines are the price of owning stocks, and we build every plan assuming there will be several more before you retire.

## Three habits we ask clients to keep

1. **Know your spending number.** If you know what you need from your portfolio each year, a 10% drop becomes a question of timing, not a crisis.
2. **Keep a cash reserve.** We suggest one to three years of planned withdrawals in short-term bonds or money market funds once you are within a few years of retirement.
3. **Rebalance, don't react.** When stocks fall, we sell some bonds and buy stocks to get back to your target mix. It rarely feels good at the time.
